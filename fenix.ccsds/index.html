Install

  1. In Fenix, press SPC h x to open the Extensions page.
  2. Search for CCSDS & ECSS, and install it. Fenix shows what it may do before it installs anything.

Or download the package and run fenix --install-extension fenix.ccsds-1.2.2.fenixext.

About

Space packets, transfer frames and CLTUs in Fenix.

  • Decode (SPC k d). The hex under the cursor, selected or on the clipboard, as a tree:

    • a space packet, through the MIB;

    • a TM, AOS or USLP frame with its packets, Reed-Solomon corrected and de-randomized as the mission says;

    • a CLTU with its TC frame;

    • a CLCW, an on-board time, or a CFDP PDU.

    Every field has its raw bytes, value and check. a reads the bytes as something else, and a field names what's behind it: a MIB definition (with the MIB extension) or a standard's heading.

  • Recordings (SPC k f). A file of packets, frames, CLTUs or records, or a text log of hex lines, as a list of packets:

    • each one named through the MIB, with lost frames and bad packets;

    • by APID;

    • a TM parameter followed through them (t), with its curve and its limit crossings;

    • what's wrong.

    w writes the filtered packets or the parameter's samples.

  • Live sources (SPC k l). The same list, filled as telemetry arrives from a source ccsds.sources lists:

    • tcp://host:port, udp://:port;

    • nats://host:port with a subject;

    • file://path, a recording still being written, in the project.

    A followed parameter says when it crosses a limit.

  • The time converter (SPC k T): an on-board time code, UTC, TAI, GPS, CDS, from the mission's epoch and its time correlation.

  • Hex view (SPC f x), and Fenix shows a binary file with it.

  • The standards (SPC k ?): the CCSDS and ECSS standards, which of them the ccsds.library folder has, and a search through all of them (with the PDF extension).

  • K (with standard keys, Ctrl+K Ctrl+I or the pointer resting) on hex or an on-board time, in any file: what it is, in short.

  • The mission clock (ccsds.clock): the time now as the mission writes it on board, in the modeline.

The mission

How the mission's bytes are laid out is its ccsds.* settings:

  • the PUS edition, time format, epoch and time correlation, checksum;

  • its frames and TC frames;

  • its virtual channels' names, live sources and checks.

A project's own settings come first. Packets are named through the MIBs the MIB extension reads (mib.roots).

For coding agents

ccsds_decode (read, Fenix 1.1) decodes bytes your coding agent gives as hex -- 1BF2C0070009, 1B F2 C0 07, 0x1B,0xF2, a hex dump with offsets -- as the packet inspector does (as says how to read them; left out, by what they look like), through the MIBs mib.roots names and against the project's mission profile. It answers the inspector's tree as JSON: each field's byte (and bit) offset, size, raw value, meaning and check, the telecommand or TM packet the MIB identified, the checks that fail and those that warn.

What it may do

  • Where you are: know where you are, and type into the file you're in (the time converter's i).

  • Files: read the project, and write into it what a stream page writes.

  • Network: connect to the live sources ccsds.sources lists. It only receives.

  • Your folders and settings: read the MIB folders, the standards folder and the MIB settings.

  • Clipboard: read the clipboard (SPC k d on copied hex).

Changelog

1.2.2

  • A command that reads a large MIB may take up to a minute, rather than being stopped after 5 seconds.

1.2.1

  • A large MIB takes about a third of the memory it did (the SCOS-2000 MIB extension's 1.2.1 reading of it), so the extension is no longer stopped for running out of memory reading one.

1.2.0

The MIB is read with the SCOS-2000 MIB extension's 1.2 reading of it:

  • Telecommands with repeated groups decode with each repetition where it is; one whose editable argument has a default in CDF_VALUE is still identified when another value was sent.

  • Variable TM packets (VPD) decode entry by entry, their groups repeated as their counters say; a parameter's calibration is chosen by its CUR rows when it has some.

  • Telecommand arguments are shown as their status texts and through their numeric calibrations; times as the UTC time or duration they are.

  • A new MIB check, layout (ccsds.checks): telecommand definitions the ICD's rules disagree with.

  • A MIB table that isn't UTF-8 is read as Latin-1 instead of being lost.

  • An XTCE file in the project can be one of its MIBs (mib.roots).

  • SPC k d on a line that starts with a name ending in digits (TTYP0001 18 64 ..., a line of the MIB's tc_vectors.txt) no longer takes the name's digits for hex.

1.1.0

For Fenix 1.1: ccsds_decode, a tool for your coding agents (read). Given bytes as hex, it decodes them as the packet inspector does -- a space packet through the MIB, a transfer frame or a CLTU with the packets it carries, a CLCW, an on-board time, a CFDP PDU -- against the project's mission profile, and answers the whole tree as JSON: each field's offset, raw value, meaning and check, the definition the MIB identified, and the checks that fail.

1.0.0

  • The first release, for Fenix 1.0: space packets, transfer frames and CLTUs decoded through the MIB, recordings and live telemetry with a parameter followed through them, on-board times, hex, and the CCSDS and ECSS standards.
