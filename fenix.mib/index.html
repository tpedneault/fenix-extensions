Install

  1. In Fenix, press SPC h x to open the Extensions page.
  2. Search for SCOS-2000 MIB, and install it. Fenix shows what it may do before it installs anything.

Or download the package and run fenix --install-extension fenix.mib-1.4.0.fenixext.

About

SCOS-2000 MIBs in Fenix. Browse a MIB, look a definition up by kind, write telecommand calls into your scripts with a form for their arguments, have the MIB check the calls you wrote, and generate code from it.

  • The MIB page (SPC k k). A tab for each kind of definition:

    • 1 telecommands, 2 TC parameters, 3 TM packets, 4 TM parameters, 5 calibrations.

    • / narrows the list by words and field:value filters, and f opens the filters as a form.

    • ! lists what's wrong in the MIBs' files, and 6 groups everything by PUS service.

    • Enter opens a definition's own page, i a telecommand's insert form.

  • A definition's page. Every name on it is a link: Enter follows it, and H/L (or Backspace) go back and forward.

  • The insert form (SPC k i, or i on a telecommand).

    • Every argument at once (CDF_ELTYPE E), each with its default (CDF_VALUE, else CPC_DEFVAL), type, unit and what it may be.

    • A counter (CDF_GRPSIZE) repeats the elements after it as often as its value says, each repetition numbered (PT[2]); +/- change the counter of the group the cursor is in.

    • v fills the form from the MIB's value sets, its default set first; a telecommand sent only while the TM shows something (PTV) says so.

    • The command is written with the project's templates, and a value outside what the MIB allows is flagged beside it.

    • I inserts it where your cursor was. SPC k e reads the call on the cursor's line back into the form, and inserting puts it back in its place.

    • b shows the packet's bytes and where each value went, y copies them and D decodes them (with the CCSDS extension).

  • In your scripts, through a language server:

    • K on a MIB name shows its card, and gd opens its page (with standard keys, Ctrl+K Ctrl+I and F12, or Ctrl+click).

    • Completion offers telecommands and parameters.

    • Telecommand calls the MIB disagrees with are underlined: unknown mnemonics, missing or out-of-range arguments, and repetitions that don't match their counter (mib.check_scripts).

    • mib.editor_files narrows which files; none listed means every file.

  • Command sequences (SPC k q): a sequence inserted as the telecommands it sends, with its formal parameters' defaults, its timing and comments as comments.

  • Two MIBs compared (SPC k D): what was added, removed and changed between two of the project's MIBs, as Markdown in generated/.

  • Generated from the MIB (SPC k g): XTCE, a Wireshark dissector, C, Python, an ICD, or TC test vectors, written to the project's generated/ -- repeated groups included.

How telecommands are read

As the SCOS-2000 Database Import ICD lays them out:

  • CDF_ELTYPE says what's asked: E elements are, F (a fixed parameter) and A (a fixed area) aren't. An E element's CDF_VALUE is its default, read raw or engineering as CDF_INTER says; D/T take it from the TM parameter CDF_TMID when it's sent.

  • A counter's CDF_GRPSIZE counts every element after it in its group -- fixed and spare ones, and a nested group's elements too.

  • CDF_BIT places each element as if every group were there once: with more repetitions, what follows moves by them; with none, back by the group's length. A variable-length value moves what follows by how much longer it is than CDF_ELLEN.

  • Raw values are in CPC_RADIX's radix; ranges in raw or engineering units as PRF_INTER says; a PRV row with no maximum allows one value. A deduced parameter (PTC 11) takes the type of the TM parameter an earlier argument (CPC_CATEG P) names.

Which MIBs

  1. The folders mib.roots names: the project's own list (in its .fenix/settings.toml, folders relative to it) in place of yours. In a project's list, an XTCE file relative to it is a MIB too (XTCE = "generated/xtce-OPS.xml").

  2. Else the project itself, when it's a MIB (.dat tables in it or in its mib/).

They're read when first needed, and again when one of their files changes (mib.watch).

The mission profile they're read and packets built against is the CCSDS settings (ccsds.pus, ccsds.tm_time, ...). The CCSDS extension keeps those; the MIB reads them.

The project doctor

In a project that's a MIB, the doctor lists:

  • its tables and rows, and their layout (ICD issue);

  • the rows with problems;

  • tables the ICD doesn't know;

  • the mission profile, and whether the MIB agrees with it and the standards;

  • its recordings.

For coding agents

mib_lookup (read, Fenix 1.1) gives your coding agents the MIBs here: a definition by name -- a telecommand's mnemonic, a TM packet's SPID or name, a calibration's id -- or by the words of its description, as / on the MIB page finds them (kind narrows it to one kind). It answers what the definition's page shows, as JSON: a telecommand's PUS type, APID and arguments, each with its kind (editable, fixed, area), bits, type, default, what it may be, its calibration and the groups it repeats in, and its value sets, pre-transmission validation and verification stages; a parameter's type, unit, calibration, limits and the packets that carry it; a packet's parameters by position; a calibration's points, texts, ranges or coefficients. When words match several, the best is answered in full and the next twenty are listed.

What it may do

  • Know where you are, and type into the file you're in (the insert form, SPC k e).

  • Read the project, and write what it generates into its generated/.

  • Read and change the MIB folders mib.roots names.

  • Read the CCSDS settings.

Changelog

1.4.0

  • The hints that named a leader sequence (SPC k k, SPC k e, SPC k ,, SPC k g, SPC k f, SPC ,) -- on a definition's card and page, in the project doctor and in what it says when there's no MIB -- say the keys the key scheme in use has: C-k k k with standard keys, your own leader chord if you set one, a key you bound yourself.

1.3.0

  • For Fenix 1.5 (extension API 1.5): a name's card says the keys of the key scheme in use for going to its page -- F12 with standard keys, gd with Vim keys. The README gives standard keys beside Vim's.

1.2.2

  • The MIB page opens on a large MIB. SPC k k read the MIB and ran its checks within the 5 seconds a command has, and a large MIB took longer: the extension was stopped ("it took too long"). A command may now take up to a minute, the checks take about a third less time (a telecommand parameter's type and calibrations are read once, not for every element using it), and a page whose MIB isn't read yet reads it in a call of its own rather than within the second a page is laid out in.

1.2.1

  • Large MIBs no longer run it out of memory. Every field of every row was its own string: a 20 MiB mission MIB took about 300 MiB once read, past the 128 MiB Fenix gives an extension, and the extension was stopped. Rows now point into their table file's text, kept once: the same MIB takes about 115 MiB, its tables alone about 40 MiB.

1.2.0

Telecommands are read the way the SCOS-2000 Database Import ICD lays them out, and the insert form, the scripts' checks and the packets built from them follow it.

  • Every parameter is asked for. Whether an element is fixed is CDF_ELTYPE's (A area, F fixed, E editable): an editable parameter with a default in CDF_VALUE used to be taken for a fixed one, left out of the form, the checks, completion, mib_lookup and the generators, and built into the packet with its default whatever was typed. CDF_VALUE is now its default, before CPC_DEFVAL, read raw or engineering as CDF_INTER / CPC_INTER say; a dynamic default names the TM parameter it comes from.

  • Repeated groups. A counter (CDF_GRPSIZE) repeats every element after it -- fixed and spare ones and nested groups included, as the ICD counts them -- so what follows a group is no longer pulled into it, the form and the packet agree, and elements are in CDF_BIT order. After a group, positions move by its extra repetitions (or back by its length when it's repeated no times); packets with groups decode with each repetition where it is, and identify by the fixed values before the first group.

  • The form: repetitions numbered (PT[2], PN · 2 of 3), +/- change the counter of the group you're in, v fills it from the MIB's value sets (CCF_DEFSET first; PST/PSV/PVS/PSM), a telecommand with pre-transmission validation (PTV) says what it waits for, and Bytes lists where each value went and where the packet disagrees with the MIB's TCP/PCDF header.

  • Values: raw values in CPC_RADIX's radix (hex without 0x), ranges in raw or engineering units as PRF_INTER says, PRV rows that allow a single value, floats checked as numbers, octets, strings (variable-length ones with their length), absolute and relative times, and deduced parameters (PTC 11) typed by the TM parameter an earlier argument names (CPC_CATEG P).

  • The scripts' checks follow the counters: a missing repetition, one value too many, a value given twice; a deduced value is checked as the parameter it names (the form shows that type too); and a note on a telecommand sent only while the TM shows something.

  • The MIB's own checks (layout, in ccsds.checks): CCF_NPARS against cdf.dat, a group running past its elements, a counter that isn't an unsigned integer, a fixed element with no value, a default or fixed value that doesn't encode or is outside its range, an element's width against its type; overlaps on telecommands with groups too.

  • A telecommand's page and K: group members under their counter, pre-transmission validation, value sets, verification stages (CVP/CVS/CVE, or the reports the acknowledgement flags ask for) and its packet header. mib_lookup answers them too.

  • Command sequences (SPC k q): a sequence inserted as the telecommands it sends, through the project's templates, with its formal parameters' defaults and its timing as comments.

  • Two MIBs compared (SPC k D): what was added, removed and changed between two of the project's MIBs, into generated/.

  • Variable TM packets (VPD) and calibration selection (CUR) when packets are decoded.

  • XTCE: groups export as repeated command containers and import back (and arrays whose length an argument gives); a MIB's own type codes, categories, radix and parameter ids go as ancillary data, so a MIB exported and read back builds the same packets, offers the same defaults (an initialValue is no longer read in the MIB's radix) and tells packets apart by their PI1/PI2 values as the MIB does. The Python, Wireshark, C and ICD generators know groups. With several MIBs, SPC k g generates from one of them, into its own file (xtce-OPS.xml): they used to go into one file, every name twice. An XTCE file in the project can be one of its MIBs (mib.roots), to read an export back. Test vectors build telecommands with octets or a parameter id and no default.

  • A .dat file that isn't UTF-8 is read as Latin-1, with a problem saying where, instead of losing the whole table; mib.columns may name the TC parameter column CPC_PNAME, as the ICD does.

1.1.0

It finds a definition by name -- a mnemonic, a SPID, a calibration's id -- or by the words of its description, as / on the MIB page does, and answers what its page shows: a telecommand's arguments with their bits, types, defaults, allowed values and calibrations; a parameter's type, calibration, limits and the packets carrying it; a packet's parameters by position; a calibration's points or texts. Other matches are listed.
