Install

  1. In Fenix, press SPC h x to open the Extensions page.
  2. Search for Image viewer, and install it. Fenix shows what it may do before it installs anything.

Or download the package and run fenix --install-extension fenix.images-1.1.0.fenixext.

What it may do

Fenix holds it to these, and shows them before installing it. Everything else is closed to it.

About

Pictures in a pane. A PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, BMP or SVG opens in it however you open it -- the explorer, the file picker, :e, a recent file, the command line, gf (with standard keys, Ctrl+click or Alt+F12) -- fitted to the pane, over the theme's background, with a checkerboard under what's transparent and a details line below: its name, format, size in pixels and on disk, how big it's shown, and its place in its folder.

KeyWhat it does
+ -Zoom in and out (no smaller than fitted)
0 =Fit the pane
1Actual size: a point a pixel, as the system shows pictures
h j k l, the arrowsPan
A drag, the wheelPan; zoom about the pointer
] [The next and previous picture in its folder, round from the last to the first
p, .Play or pause a moving picture; the next frame, paused
sAn SVG's source: its text, to edit
dHide or show the details line
rRead it again (it changed on disk)
qClose it

These are the diagram viewer's keys (SPC m v in a Mermaid file). Zoomed in three times or more, each pixel is drawn as a square, to see them; shown smaller, it's drawn from a picture halved as often as it takes, so fine detail doesn't shimmer. An SVG is drawn again at every zoom, so it's sharp however close you look, and it fits the pane whatever its size; its text is set in the system's fonts.

GIFs, animated WebPs and APNGs play, a frame at a time, at their own pace (a frame asking for 10 ms or less is shown for 100 ms, as browsers do), and stop while they're out of sight.

SPC m on a picture has the same, and SPC f i (Ctrl+K f i with standard keys) from anywhere previews the picture the line you're on names -- ![plot](img/plot.png), src="logo.svg", "sprites/cat.gif"; the one under the cursor, when it names more -- in a pane beside it, the focus staying in your code. Again on the same picture closes it. A picture in Markdown's reading view opens here too (Enter on it).

SPC f o goes from a picture to its file's text, and back.

KeyCommandWhat it does
SPC f iimages.previewThe picture this line names, beside it
SPC m ] SPC m [images.next images.previousThe next and previous picture in its folder
SPC m 0 SPC m 1images.fit images.actual_sizeFit, actual size
SPC m simages.sourceAn SVG's source, to edit
SPC m pimages.playPlay or pause
SPC m dimages.detailsThe details line

The modeline says the same as the details line, for the picture looked at last: IMG cat.gif · GIF · 640 × 480 · 1.2 MB · playing, 12 frames · fit 50% · 3/12.

It needs Fenix 1.5 or later (extension API 1.5). What it says names the keys that run a command in the key scheme you use, and yours where you rebound them: SPC f i, or C-k f i with standard keys.

Limits

  • Big pictures. An extension has 128 MiB, so a picture is decoded whole up to about 8.4 megapixels (a 4K screenshot). A bigger JPEG is decoded at a half, a quarter or an eighth of its size (the details line says so), which the pane doesn't miss until you zoom in past fitted; a bigger picture of another kind isn't shown, and says why.

  • Its folder. ] and [ go through the folder a picture's in. One outside the project Fenix lists for it, and only the pictures in it.

  • The session. A picture opens again with the session, at the zoom you left it: Fenix gives the extension again the files its pages show, outside the project too.

  • SVG source. s opens the file's own text, to edit; r on the picture reads it again once it's saved. An SVGZ is compressed, so its s is a copy, read-only. SPC f o goes to the text of the file the pane was opened on, not the one ] went to.

  • The preview reads pictures in the project.

What it may do

  • Read the project you're in: a picture's folder, for ] and [, and the pictures a line names, for the preview. Outside it, the pictures beside one you opened, which Fenix lists for it.

  • Know where you are in the editor, for the preview: the file, the line, the cursor and its project.

  • Read the system's fonts, for an SVG's text and the details line.

Building

cargo xtask pack extensions/images dist

cargo test checks the arithmetic of zooming and panning, the folder's order, what a file is from its bytes, the pictures a line names, and the pixels drawn: the checks, the theme, squares and halving, an SVG's edges.
